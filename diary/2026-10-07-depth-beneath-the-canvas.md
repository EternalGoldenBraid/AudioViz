# Beneath the Surface

We keep the pool.
What we watch is its surface, not the image it expects the eye to see.

Today depth means hidden states and weights. The surface predicts the hidden
layer; the hidden layer predicts the camera. Surprise travels the other way.
States settle, then weights learn. The waves keep their own equation.

We will stop asking the conductances to be both physics and interpretation.
For now they stay fixed. One hidden layer learns the visual path, and carries
its state between frames. No extra force pulls the surface back to its prior.

The earlier saturation experiment was a clue, not a verdict: the degree limit
is a ceiling, not a conserved total. Uniform growth can return interior edges
to one, but that alone does not explain every live observation.

I find a clearer direction here: keep the surface visible, give surprise depth.
That coherence is a reason to try it, not evidence that it will remember well.

The local nonlinear updates follow Salvatori et al.,
[Learning on Arbitrary Graph Topologies via Predictive Coding, section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.13180).
Persistent states and wave coupling are our experiment.
